# Human rating manual — GenAI exposure of NCO-2015 task statements

Rubric version {{RUBRIC_VERSION}} · sheet built {{BUILT}} · generated by `src/analysis/validation_sheet.py`

## 1. Why you are doing this

The AI Exposure Atlas scores every task statement in India's National
Classification of Occupations (NCO-2015, Vol II) for exposure to generative
AI. A language model produced the working labels. The paper cannot use them
until independent human raters have scored a validation sample and agreement
with the model is high enough (Cohen's κ ≥ {{MIN_KAPPA}}, Golden Rule 4 of the
project spec). Two earlier pilot rounds failed that bar (κ 0.59, then 0.54).
Your ratings decide whether the index stands, and where the rubric still needs
work. Rate carefully; do not rate quickly.

## 3. Procedure

1. **Independence.** Each rater works alone on their own copy of the sheet and
   fills only their own `rating_n` and `notes_n` columns. No discussion of
   individual rows with the other raters until all three copies are returned.
   The copies are merged afterwards.
2. **Blind.** Do not look up the model's labels, the atlas page, or any
   published exposure score for the occupation. Do not search the web for
   whether "AI can do X"; judge from the rubric and your own knowledge of
   tools available in 2025-26.
3. **Order.** Work top to bottom by `seq`. Do not skip ahead to easier rows.
4. **Allowed values** in `rating_n`: exactly `E0`, `E1`, `E2` or `NA`.
   Nothing else. Blank means not yet rated. Upper case, no spaces.
5. **Pace.** Read the statement twice, apply the flow in §4, decide. Typical
   time is under a minute per row. If a row takes more than two minutes,
   record your best answer and say why in `notes_n`; do not leave it blank.
6. **Notes.** Optional except where §4 or §5 asks for a tag. Cite the rubric
   rule you leaned on when the call was close ("Rule 8", "Rule 9").
7. **Do not edit** any column other than your two. Do not re-order, sort,
   filter-and-save, or delete rows. Save as CSV, UTF-8, same filename with
   your rater number appended (`rating_sheet_v1_rater2.csv`).
8. **Sessions.** Rate in sittings of at most 100 rows. Note the sitting
   boundaries in `notes_n` on the first row of each sitting ("sitting 3") so
   fatigue effects can be checked.

## 4. Decision flow

Apply the rubric (§6) in this order for every statement.

1. **Is this a task at all?** If the text is a designation line ("Is
   designated according to work performed", "May be designated as…", "May
   specialise in…"), a bare title in capitals, a machine name, or a fragment
   with no activity, enter `NA` and write `R-04` (designation line) or
   `R-05` (fragment) in notes. `NA` rows are excluded from agreement
   statistics and counted separately.
2. **What is the essence of the activity as written?** Score the statement,
   not the occupation (Rule 1). If several activities are bundled, score the
   one that takes most of the time in the statement.
3. **Is the essence physical, on-site, or the exercise of authority over
   people?** Manual work, handling goods, cash, tools, crops, animals or
   people; operating vehicles or machines; inspection or enumeration that
   requires being there; supervising, coordinating, chairing, negotiating.
   If yes → `E0` (Rules 3, 4, 6, 8). Stop.
4. **Could a chat window alone halve the time at equal quality?** Every
   input and output named in the statement is text the worker could type or
   paste in and read out: drafting, summarising, translating, replying to
   written queries, preparing plans, notes, reports, analysis, code (without
   running it). If yes → `E1` (Rule 9). Stop.
5. **Could attainable LLM-powered software halve it?** The statement needs
   speech in or out, paper documents or images, an organisation's records or
   systems, form-filling, or running something. Count today's consumer and
   business tools: speech-to-text, OCR, document Q&A, spreadsheet and database
   assistants, code execution. Do not count robots, physical automation or
   future systems. If yes → `E2` (Rule 5, Rule 9). Stop.
6. Otherwise → `E0`.

Quality bar throughout: "equivalent quality" means the customer or employer
would not notice a drop (Rule 7). Language is never the barrier (Rule 2).

## 5. Known hard cases

These patterns caused most of the disagreement in the pilot rounds. Rate them
under the rubric as it stands, and add the tag so each pattern's disagreement
can be counted when the rubric is revised.

| Pattern | Rate as | Tag in notes |
|---|---|---|
| Statement is about directing or supervising people, but a plan, roster or report is mentioned | `E0` unless the statement is primarily about producing that document (Rule 8) | `R-01` |
| Text task that in practice would use records, documents or dictation, but the statement names only text inputs | `E1` (Rule 9: only what is *named* counts) | `R-02` |
| Statement names only an input act with no content: "May do his own typing", "Enters data", "Keys in figures", "Copies documents" | Apply Rules 1 and 9 strictly as written; the rubric has no explicit rule yet, so your judgement is the data | `R-03` |
| Designation or specialisation boilerplate | `NA` | `R-04` |
| Title fragment, machine name, no activity | `NA` | `R-05` |

## 7. After rating

The three copies are merged into one sheet and `make validation-kappa` writes
`outputs/validation/validation_kappa_report.json`: each rater against the
model, the human majority against the model (the gate statistic), Fleiss' κ
across the three raters, per-stratum agreement, and counts of `NA` rows and
`R-` tags. Disagreements go to `config/RUBRIC_REVISION_NOTES.md` for the next
rubric version. No task label is ever edited by hand.
